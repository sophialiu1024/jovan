Please cancel this partnership!

Thank you so much,

Origin at (0,0)

Ilya,

I don’t do much history, but you’re better at nagging people than actually doing proper work in your department, idiot. I don’t have time for this, and I don’t have much information for you anyway. But. In the boat that was bound for a big city of trade, they hit an iceberg and landed in the northernmost port that eventually became Romanov-on-Murman, there were six passengers, including Romanov, Ekaterina, Mariya, and who we now know as the Zvevda. Two of them—their names weren’t recorded. If they were, I would’ve found them—died of the cold. The tundra to the south was too harsh to pass through, and there were mountains that were near impossible to climb. However, Mariya managed to leave on foot. The three who stayed behind—Romanov, Ekaterina and the Zvevda, as you’d likely forgotten—decided to begin civilization here. The starlight saw their efforts and blessed them. I’ll leave the rest up to you.

Sofia

Friend's Letter

Dearest Mariya,

I know by now that my letters will never reach you. But I am writing anyway. I miss you, Masha. Romanov and I are well, and…he…he is the same as he always has been. It’s not the same here without you. It’s so, so cold. I feel my bones rattling with the wind. But there is good news along with bad news. Little Inna is growing up so fast. She can walk already, and is always calling for me. I wish you could have gotten to see her. She would’ve loved her Aunt Masha.

The starlight here is warm. I don’t know if I have to thank…him…for this, but Romanov insists we do. He’s always believed in peace, after all. And I do too. Masha, I hope you are doing well down south. Is it warmer there? I have not seen a bird in years. I know you love them.

Lots of love and well wishes,
Katya.
